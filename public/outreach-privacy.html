How we got your details

Last updated 19 September 2026

You are reading this because Belline wrote to you at work and you did not ask us to. This page is the notice you are owed under Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR: what we hold about you, where we found it, why we think we are allowed to use it, who else sees it, how long we keep it, and how to make it stop. It takes one click to make it stop, and we do not ask why.

If you want this to end now: use the unsubscribe link in the footer of the email we sent you, or write one word to hello@belline.ai. Either one is enough. Nothing else is needed from you, and you do not have to give a reason.

Who is processing your data

The controller is Belline, reachable at hello@belline.ai. Represented by —. Registered under —.

For anything on this page, write to hello@belline.ai. A person reads that address. We have not appointed a data protection officer; we are too small to be required to, and the address above reaches the people who decide these things.

What we hold about you

  • Your business email address, and your name where the address or the listing gave one.
  • Your role at the business, where it was published.
  • The business itself: its name, address, telephone number, website, country, opening hours and the kind of work it does.
  • Short notes we wrote about the business from reading its website — what it offers, and what about its way of taking bookings made us think Belline would be useful to it. These are our own observations, not facts about you personally.
  • A record of what we sent you and when, and whether you opened it, clicked, replied, or told us to stop.

We do not hold special-category data about you. We do not buy lists, we do not guess email addresses by pattern, and we do not use tracking pixels that identify you.

Where it came from

We did not get it from you. Two sources only:

  • Public business listings — directories, maps entries and professional registers where a business publishes its contact details so that people can reach it.
  • Your own website — the contact, imprint or team page, read automatically.

Because we collected it about you rather than from you, Article 14 applies, and this page is our notice under it. The first email we send you links here.

Why we believe we may use it

Our legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR — legitimate interests. The interest, stated plainly and without dressing it up: we are a new company trying to find its first customers, and writing to businesses that look like they need what we sell is cheaper for us than advertising. That is a commercial interest of ours, not a benefit to you. Recital 47 GDPR recognises direct marketing as capable of being a legitimate interest; it does not say it always is.

We weighed that against your side of it, and these are the things we do to keep the intrusion small. They are commitments, not decoration:

  • We write to a business address about that business's work — never to a private address, and never about anything but our own product.
  • A short sequence and then silence: at most a handful of messages, spaced days apart, and never more than a small number to one business in any ninety days. The limits are enforced in our software, not left to whoever is sending.
  • One reply, one click, or one word, and we stop for good — the whole business, every address at it, not just the one that answered.
  • We do not profile you, score you as an individual, or make automated decisions that affect you.
  • We do not share your details with anyone who would market to you.

Where this is uncertain, and we would rather say so. Whether an unsolicited commercial email is lawful does not turn on the GDPR alone. In Germany § 7 UWG, in Austria § 107 TKG and in Switzerland Art. 3 UWG each impose their own, stricter rule on sending it at all, and our reading of them for business recipients has not been tested by a court or by a regulator. If you think we have it wrong in your case, we would genuinely like to hear it at hello@belline.ai, and your objection is effective whether or not we agree with your reasoning.

Nobody else. We do not sell your details, do not rent them, and do not pass them to other advertisers. Both providers process data outside the United Arab Emirates, including in the United States and the European Union; for transfers out of the EEA we rely on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses in our agreements with them.

The text you send us if you reply is read by a person here, not published, and used only to answer you and to stop the sequence.

How long we keep it

  • If you never reply: the sequence ends, and we delete your contact details and our notes within twelve months of the last message we sent you.
  • If you reply and we end up talking: we keep the correspondence for as long as the conversation is live, and for two years after it goes quiet, in case you come back.
  • If you become a customer: the customer privacy policy takes over from this page.
  • If you object or unsubscribe: see the next section. That record outlives all of the above, on purpose.

Objecting, and the suppression list

Under Article 21(2) GDPR you may object to processing for direct marketing at any time. It is unconditional: you do not have to justify it, and we do not get to weigh anything against it. Once you object we must stop, and we do.

How, in one step: click the unsubscribe link in the footer of any email from us, or send any message at all to hello@belline.ai. The link works without signing in, without a form and without a reason. It takes effect immediately, and it applies to your whole business — every address at it, not only the one you used.

What we then keep, and why. Saying stop puts your business on a permanent suppression list, and we keep the minimum needed to honour it: your email address, your business, the date, and the fact that it was an objection. We keep nothing else, and we never write to you again.

We keep it because deleting the record is how a business ends up written to a second time by the next import of the same public listing. Article 17(3)(b) and (e) allow us to retain what is needed for compliance with a legal obligation and for the establishment or exercise of legal claims, and Article 21(3) requires us to stop processing for marketing — which we cannot reliably do without remembering who said stop. Marked as uncertain: the exact basis for retaining a suppression record is a point on which supervisory authorities have not all said the same thing, and a lawyer should look at it before we send at volume. What is not uncertain is the alternative: forgetting means writing to you again, which is the harm you asked us to prevent. If you would rather we erased the suppression record entirely, say so and we will, and we will tell you plainly that we may then have no way of knowing not to contact you again.

Your other rights

You can ask us for any of the following, and we answer within one month:

  • Access (Art. 15) — a copy of everything we hold about you, including our own notes.
  • Rectification (Art. 16) — correction of anything wrong.
  • Erasure (Art. 17) — deletion, subject only to the suppression record above.
  • Restriction (Art. 18) — that we hold it but stop using it while something is disputed.
  • Portability (Art. 20) — limited here, because our basis is legitimate interests rather than consent or contract, but ask and we will send you the data in a readable file anyway.
  • Objection (Art. 21) — as above.

One address for all of them: hello@belline.ai. We do not require you to prove your identity to unsubscribe. We may ask for something only where a request would otherwise hand your data to somebody else.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection supervisory authority — in the EU, the one for the country you live or work in, or where you think the problem happened. You do not have to raise it with us first, though we would rather you did.
