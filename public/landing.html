The phone rings

One receptionist. Three trades that book nothing like each other.

A table is not an appointment. A restaurant fills a room to a pacing limit; a salon and a clinic fill a person's diary, with the right qualification and the right room. Belline knows the difference, and speaks in each trade's own words.

Restaurants

Turn times by party size, the tightest table that fits, two tables combined when they're in the same section, and a cap on covers per slot so the kitchen isn't handed twelve tables at eight o'clock.

Salons & spas

Only stylists qualified for the service are offered. Chains of services run back to back, colour stations are shared, and the cleanup buffer is held after the guest's time without ever being quoted to them.

Clinics & dental

Practitioners, surgeries and treatment rooms as separate constraints. A first visit books as a consultation, never as the treatment itself. It will not discuss a diagnosis, and anything urgent goes to a human immediately.

Something else? Anything that books people into time — veterinary, physiotherapy, opticians, driving schools, tattoo studios, garages — runs on the same engine. The vocabulary and the rules are configuration, not new code. Tell us your trade.

It checks what is genuinely free

Answering is the easy part. Knowing what's actually free is not.

A voice agent that can't see your book is an expensive answering machine. Belline holds the same constraints your host or receptionist holds in their head, which is why it can commit to a time without anyone checking it afterwards.

Restaurants

  • ✓ Turn times that grow with the party — a deuce is ninety minutes, a six-top is two hours
  • ✓ Kitchen pacing caps covers seated per quarter hour, however many tables look empty
  • ✓ A party of two takes the smallest table that fits, never the six-top you'll want at nine
  • ✓ Large parties combine tables, but only within the same section
  • ✓ Last seating respected; a nine-top becomes an events callback, not a booking

Salons and clinics

  • ✓ Only staff qualified for every service in the chain are offered
  • ✓ Cleanup buffer held in the diary after each appointment, never quoted to the guest
  • ✓ Colour stations treated as a real resource — two colour clients need two of them
  • ✓ A three-hour balayage is never booked into a shift that ends in two
  • ✓ Work spreads across the team instead of stacking on whoever is listed first

A voice minute is time Belline spends on a live call with your caller — from the moment it answers to the moment the call ends — rounded up to the next whole minute. Calls you make from your own test console do not count, and neither do calls cut short by a fault on our side.

Questions

What operators ask first.

Do we have to change our phone number?

No. You keep the number on your signage, your listing and your menus. You set call forwarding on the line you already have, and you can turn it off yourself at any time.

Will it answer calls our staff would have taken?

Only if you want it to. Most venues start with forwarding on no-answer and busy, so the phone rings at the host stand exactly as it does today and Belline only picks up calls that would otherwise have gone to voicemail.

Can it double-book us?

It cannot state a time without checking availability first, and availability is computed from your real tables, turn times and pacing — or your staff, service durations and equipment. If a slot isn't genuinely free, it isn't offered.

What happens when it doesn't know something?

It says so, and takes a message or transfers to a person. It is instructed never to invent a price, a dish, a product or a policy. You define the house rules it must follow, and it treats them as hard constraints.

Does it sound like a robot?

You choose the voice and hear it saying your venue's own name before you commit. It replies in under a second, and it stops talking the moment a caller interrupts — which is the thing people actually notice.

How long does setup take?

About thirty minutes on a call, going through your tables or your service list, your hours, your policies and your common questions. Changes after that take effect on the next call — there is no waiting on a release.

Are callers told they're speaking to an AI?

Yes, in the greeting, and we recommend it everywhere. In the EU it is a legal requirement. It is also simply better: callers who know speak more clearly and are less annoyed when they find out.

What do you do with our guests' data?

Transcripts and bookings are stored so you can audit what was said; call audio is not recorded. The data belongs to your venue and is exportable. We are happy to sign whatever your legal counsel requires.

What does it cost?

AED 179, AED 365 or AED 899 a month per venue, depending on how busy your phone is. Minutes are included in the plan and there is no per-minute charge on top of any of them. No setup fee. The full breakdown is above.

What happens if we run out of minutes?

Belline keeps answering. A receptionist that stops picking up because of an invoice is not a receptionist, so we do not build one — your dashboard tells you that you have gone past, and which plan would cover it.

You are never charged a penny more than the plan fee for that. Moving up takes effect straight away, and if your phone is busy enough that counting is a nuisance, Enterprise is unlimited.

Are we tied in?

No. Monthly plans are cancelled whenever you like and run to the end of the period you have paid for. Annual is two months free in exchange for paying up front. Standard onboarding costs nothing.
