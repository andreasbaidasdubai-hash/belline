The honest read. Every remaining risk is commercial, not technical. We do not know what a Dubai clinic pays today, whether they would switch, or whether the UAE telephony problem is solvable at all. Building more features before answering those is the most expensive mistake available right now.

Week 1 Goal: find out if anyone wants this — before spending another hour building

Talk to ten clinics.

  1. Call ten dental and aesthetic clinics in Dubai

    Not to sell. To ask five questions: who answers your phone, how many calls do you think you miss, what happens to a missed call, what do you pay that person, and would you pay for something that caught them.

    Why first: every financial number in the market study is an assumption — call volume, handling time, price. One afternoon of calls replaces all of them. If eight of ten say they never miss calls, the whole thesis is wrong and you have saved three months.

  2. Get the three API keys and hear it yourself

    Anthropic, Deepgram, ElevenLabs into .env, then npm run dev and press Speak in the test console. Around $40 covers all of it.

    Why now: you have never actually heard this thing talk. You cannot sell it, price it, or judge whether it is good enough until you have had a conversation with it.

  3. Pick the voice and read the clinic prompt aloud

    On the Agent page, switch to Meridian Dental & Aesthetics, try voices with Hear it, and read the compiled system prompt end to end.

    Why: the medical guardrails are written but never pressure-tested. Ask it about chest pain, about whether a filling is covered by insurance, about someone else's appointment. Fix what it gets wrong before a real patient asks.

Week 2 Goal: a number you can hand to a prospect

Make it callable.

  1. Deploy and claim the owner account

    Follow the go-live runbook: GitHub, Railway, a mounted volume at /data, then open the URL and create the owner account immediately — that form is open until somebody uses it.

  2. Buy a UK number and point it at the webhook

    About $1.15 a month, live in a minute, no documentation. Anyone in the UAE dials it as a normal international call.

    Why not a UAE number: Twilio does not sell them — the TDRA reserves inbound VoIP for Etisalat and du. This is the single biggest unknown in the plan, which is why step 7 starts now rather than later.

  3. Add an SMS-capable number

    Set TWILIO_SMS_FROM and every booking gets a written confirmation automatically.

    Why it matters more than it sounds: a reference read aloud gets written down wrong, and the caller rings back to check — which costs the venue the exact call the agent just saved them.

Weeks 2–6 Goal: turn the regulatory wall from a blocker into a moat

Solve UAE telephony, in parallel with everything else.

  1. Open conversations with UAE-licensed providers now

    Etisalat and du business divisions, and any TDRA-licensed SIP trunk reseller. The question is narrow: can we terminate inbound calls to a UAE number on our own infrastructure, and what does it take.

    Why it is the whole strategy: the same rule that blocks us blocks Slang.ai, Retell and every US-built competitor. If we solve it we own a market they cannot casually enter. If it turns out to be unsolvable, we learn that in week three rather than month six.

  2. Sell forwarding meanwhile — it works today

    A clinic keeps its number and sets conditional forwarding on no-answer and busy. Nothing about their setup changes.

    Confirm the international forwarding rate with the venue's operator before promising anything. Forwarding a UAE landline abroad puts an international call on their bill.

Weeks 4–8 Goal: three clinics using it on real calls, free

Pilot with three, not thirty.

  1. Give it away to three clinics

    Free, for eight weeks, in exchange for honest feedback and permission to use them as a reference. Configure each one properly with the practice manager: treatments, durations, practitioners, rooms, policies, common questions. Budget half an hour each.

  2. Read every transcript, every morning

    All of them, for the first month. Each one shows you a policy nobody thought to write down, a question you have no answer for, or a phrase the agent uses that a clinic would never use.

    Why this is the real work: the difference between a demo and a product is roughly two hundred small corrections that only real callers reveal. Nothing else in this plan is as valuable per hour.

  3. Replace every assumption with a measurement

    Calls per month, average handling time, containment rate, bookings per hundred calls, cost per call. The dashboard already records all of it.

Weeks 8–12 Goal: the first invoice

Charge someone.

  1. Price from the pilot data, not from the landing page

    You will know real minute volumes and real cost per venue. Price at a clear multiple of what a missed booking is worth to them — which the week-one calls told you.

  2. Build exactly one integration — the one a paying customer demands

    The seam is BookingBackend. Do not build speculatively against four systems; build the one a clinic makes a condition of paying.

  3. Harden what a paying customer implies

    Postgres instead of the JSON store, password reset by email, and two-factor. All three are avoidable while it is a pilot and indefensible once you hold real patient data for money.

What not to do.

  • Do not lead with salons. Fresha is bundling an AI Belline at roughly $80 a location into software salons already pay for. Keep the capability; do not build a go-to-market around it.
  • Do not enter DACH yet. Parloa raised $350M in January 2026 at a $3B valuation and Fonio is a well-reviewed German-native specialist. Switzerland is a second market because of your entity, not a first one.
  • Do not build outbound calling yet. No-show recovery is the obvious next feature and it is worthless until inbound is proven with real venues.
  • Do not add languages before English is right. Gulf Arabic dialect handling is a genuine differentiator and a genuine quality bar — it deserves its own project once there is revenue to justify it.
  • Do not deploy publicly and walk away. The first-run setup form creates the owner account for whoever loads it first.
